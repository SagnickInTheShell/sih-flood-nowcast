## Deliberate critical-infrastructure siting

The hospital and fire station are **deliberately** sited to be vulnerable --
this is a design choice, not a rigged demo. Real critical facilities do get
built near a natural drainage low point and served by only two access
roads; the synthetic ward models that specific, well-documented urban-
planning failure mode directly, so it shows up honestly through the same
pipeline every other scenario runs through (no special-cased "flood the
hospital" logic anywhere).

**What's actually modeled:**
- The anti-diagonal arterial runs almost exactly along the depression's
  centreline (both are the `row+col≈99` line by construction), so the 4
  major drainage junctions placed along it sit in the deepest part of the
  carved channel.
- Of those 4, empirically (verified against the physics baseline directly,
  not assumed) only **one** genuinely becomes the network's true
  topological sink -- the point strictly lower than every one of its own
  neighbours once terrain noise is applied. The other 3 drain onward fine
  regardless of how deep the channel is carved, because ponding depth is
  driven by delivered inflow volume divided by catchment area once a node
  *is* a sink, not by how deep the pit under it is.
- So the hospital and fire station are both sited near that **one** real
  chokepoint (node `n_3_4` in the synthetic ward's node ids), each served
  by two access roads (`add_access_spur` in `synthetic_ward.py`'s
  `_road_graph`) that both terminate there -- one direct, one via a short
  captive service lane with no other connection. This models "a small
  civic cluster built near the ward's one bridge over the nullah, with a
  front entrance and a service road that both cross the same crossing" --
  not two independently-routed roads that coincidentally both flood.
- The access spurs use the smaller residential pipe diameter, compounding
  the vulnerability (their own capacity backs up before the arterial does).
- The shelter is deliberately left with its original, better-connected
  placement on the far side of the ward: real disaster-planning guidance
  sites emergency shelters on safer ground, away from a single chokepoint,
  and the ward models that contrast on purpose.

**Tuning history** (why the numbers are what they are, not the build
spec's original figures): a -2m channel carve over a 7m base-slope drop
left a grid corner unrelated to the channel as the ward's lowest point
instead of the channel itself. Fixing that (deepening the carve, softening
the base slope) made the channel the dominant low-lying feature, but the
GNN surrogate's mean prediction still ran measurably below the
deterministic physics baseline at the chokepoint (a real, documented
surrogate-approximation gap -- see `metrics.json`'s MAE/RMSE), landing
right on the 0.30m flooded-threshold boundary rather than clearing it
reliably. The dense-settlement curve number and the 1400mm trunk
interceptor diameter were the two changes that actually moved the
*predicted* depth (not just the baseline depth) with a comfortable,
repeatable margin -- verified across 8 repeated stochastic MC-dropout
inference runs at the "Heavy cloudburst" preset, not just once.

**Result** (verified live, `PILOT_MODE=synthetic`, seed=42): at the
"Moderate storm" preset (60mm/hr) both facilities still show
`access_redundancy_score: 2`, with the chokepoint's roads only `at_risk`.
Between 60mm/hr and 80mm/hr the chokepoint crosses the flooded threshold
and both facilities' `access_redundancy_score` drops to `0`, populating
`critical_access_risk` in `POST /api/route` whenever the destination is
the hospital or fire station -- reliably reproduced at the "Heavy
cloudburst" preset (100mm/hr), already in the shipped demo scenarios, no
unrealistic rainfall values required.

## Adding a real historical event for backtesting

`GET /api/backtest/{event_id}` returns `501` until a real event is added.
To add one:

1. Find a documented flood event for your target ward (e.g. a municipal
   disaster-management report, a news report with a specific date/location,
   or IMD rainfall records) with a source URL.
2. Add a `HistoricalEvent` to `backend/app/ml/backtest.py`'s `EVENTS` dict
   with real `date`, `location_note`, `rainfall_timeseries_mm_hr`,
   `reported_flooded_streets`, and `source_url` values.
3. `run_backtest()` is already fully wired to run it through the same
   prediction pipeline used for live nowcasting.

## Adding a real pilot ward

1. Pick a bounding box (`min_lon,min_lat,max_lon,max_lat`) for your target
   ward (~2km x 2km recommended, matching the synthetic ward's scale).
2. Set `PILOT_MODE=real` and `REAL_WARD_BBOX=<your bbox>` in `.env`.
3. Run `python scripts/download_srtm.py --bbox <your bbox>` and follow its
   printed instructions (requires a free USGS EarthExplorer account).
4. Restart the backend -- `RealWardProvider` will pull roads/critical
   infrastructure from OpenStreetMap live and read the DEM tile from disk.
