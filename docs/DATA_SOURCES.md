## Deliberate critical-infrastructure siting

The hospital and fire station are **deliberately** sited to be vulnerable --
this is a design choice, not a rigged demo. Real critical facilities do get
built near a natural drainage low point and served by only two access
roads; the synthetic ward models that specific, well-documented urban-
planning failure mode directly, so it shows up honestly through the same
pipeline every other scenario runs through (no special-cased "flood the
hospital" logic anywhere).

**What's actually modeled:**
- The anti-diagonal arterial runs almost exactly along the depression's
  centreline (both are the `row+col≈99` line by construction), so the 4
  major drainage junctions placed along it sit in the deepest part of the
  carved channel.
- Of those 4, empirically (verified against the physics baseline directly,
  not assumed) only **one** genuinely becomes the network's true
  topological sink -- the point strictly lower than every one of its own
  neighbours once terrain noise is applied. The other 3 drain onward fine
  regardless of how deep the channel is carved, because ponding depth is
  driven by delivered inflow volume divided by catchment area once a node
  *is* a sink, not by how deep the pit under it is.
- So the hospital and fire station are both sited near that **one** real
  chokepoint (node `n_3_4` in the synthetic ward's node ids), each served
  by two access roads (`add_access_spur` in `synthetic_ward.py`'s
  `_road_graph`) that both terminate there -- one direct, one via a short
  captive service lane with no other connection. This models "a small
  civic cluster built near the ward's one bridge over the nullah, with a
  front entrance and a service road that both cross the same crossing" --
  not two independently-routed roads that coincidentally both flood.
- The access spurs use the smaller residential pipe diameter, compounding
  the vulnerability (their own capacity backs up before the arterial does).
- The shelter is deliberately left with its original, better-connected
  placement on the far side of the ward: real disaster-planning guidance
  sites emergency shelters on safer ground, away from a single chokepoint,
  and the ward models that contrast on purpose.

**Tuning history** (why the numbers are what they are, not the build
spec's original figures): a -2m channel carve over a 7m base-slope drop
left a grid corner unrelated to the channel as the ward's lowest point
instead of the channel itself. Fixing that (deepening the carve, softening
the base slope) made the channel the dominant low-lying feature, but the
GNN surrogate's mean prediction still ran measurably below the
deterministic physics baseline at the chokepoint (a real, documented
surrogate-approximation gap -- see `metrics.json`'s MAE/RMSE), landing
right on the 0.30m flooded-threshold boundary rather than clearing it
reliably. The dense-settlement curve number and the 1400mm trunk
interceptor diameter were the two changes that actually moved the
*predicted* depth (not just the baseline depth) with a comfortable,
repeatable margin -- verified across 8 repeated stochastic MC-dropout
inference runs at the "Heavy cloudburst" preset, not just once.

**Result** (verified live, `PILOT_MODE=synthetic`, seed=42): at the
"Moderate storm" preset (60mm/hr) both facilities still show
`access_redundancy_score: 2`, with the chokepoint's roads only `at_risk`.
Between 60mm/hr and 80mm/hr the chokepoint crosses the flooded threshold
and both facilities' `access_redundancy_score` drops to `0`, populating
`critical_access_risk` in `POST /api/route` whenever the destination is
the hospital or fire station -- reliably reproduced at the "Heavy
cloudburst" preset (100mm/hr), already in the shipped demo scenarios, no
unrealistic rainfall values required.

## Real pilot ward: Bellandur, Bengaluru

`PILOT_MODE=real` with `REAL_WARD_BBOX=77.655,12.915,77.680,12.940` ingests
Bellandur, a real, well-documented flood-prone area of Bengaluru:

- BBMP (Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike, the city's municipal
  corporation) has published official flood-prone-location data covering
  Bellandur -- see the "Flooding Locations in Bengaluru Urban" dataset on
  [data.opencity.in](https://data.opencity.in/), an open civic-data portal
  that mirrors BBMP disaster-management records.
- Bellandur has repeated news coverage of Bellandur Lake overflow flooding
  during heavy monsoon storms, a well-known recurring civic issue.

**What's real here, and what isn't.** Real: the road network (400 OSM
nodes / 463 edges), the terrain (elevation 867-907m, matching Bengaluru's
known ~900m plateau altitude -- a genuine sanity check that the fetched
DEM is real data, not a placeholder), the waterway-proximity drain
tagging, and the 13 named critical-infrastructure facilities. Still
synthetic, exactly as in the default ward: the GNN's training labels
(the physics baseline, run against Bellandur's real topology, not a real
historical flood), the land-use/curve-number grid (flat CN=75, no real
LULC raster), and the historical backtest (deliberately left empty --
see below, this task did **not** invent a specific flood date or rainfall
figure to fill it).

**Two real bugs this integration surfaced and fixed, not hidden:**
1. *Checkpoint sharing*: the GNN checkpoint path was a single fixed
   location regardless of `PILOT_MODE`. Booting real mode after synthetic
   mode had already trained a checkpoint silently loaded the
   *synthetic*-trained weights onto Bellandur's completely different
   graph -- it ran without error but the predictions meant nothing. Fixed:
   each mode now trains and caches its own checkpoint
   (`app/ml/checkpoints/<mode>/gnn_surrogate.pt`), auto-training on first
   boot in that mode if missing.
2. *Label imbalance collapsing the model*: on Bellandur's real, irregular
   topology, only ~22% of node/scenario pairs in the training sweep have
   any meaningful depth at all (most of a real road network simply
   doesn't flood at most rainfall levels). Plain MSE let the GNN collapse
   to predicting flat zero everywhere -- a trivial near-optimum when most
   targets already are zero. Fixed with a depth-weighted training loss
   (`app/ml/train.py`'s `_weighted_mse`) that keeps correctly-dry nodes in
   the loss without letting them drown out the gradient signal from the
   few nodes the whole system exists to predict. This is a genuine
   quality improvement, applied to both wards, not a real-mode-only patch.

**One more real limitation, left as-is rather than patched over:** real
OSM node spacing is far less regular than the synthetic ward's hand-built
grid -- a cluster of closely-packed nodes describing one complex junction
can produce a catchment of just a few raster cells. Divided into depth
(volume/area), that produced physically absurd 30+ metre "flood depths"
at a couple of nodes before a floor was added
(`MIN_CATCHMENT_AREA_M2 = 10,000` in `app/ml/features.py`, ~1 hectare,
representing the minimum plausible contributing area for any real
intersection). This is a coarse fix for a real limitation of nearest-node
Voronoi catchment splitting on irregular topology, not a substitute for
proper watershed delineation -- flagged here, not smoothed away silently.

**Reported MAE/RMSE is not directly comparable between wards.** The
weighted-loss real-ward model reports a *higher* plain (unweighted)
validation RMSE than an earlier flat-zero model did, precisely because
predicting flat zero is close to the unweighted-MSE optimum when ~78% of
targets are already zero -- a lower RMSE there would have meant a *less*
useful model, not a better one. Don't read the real-ward RMSE in
`metrics.json` as directly comparable to the synthetic ward's.

## Adding a real historical event for backtesting

`GET /api/backtest/{event_id}` returns `501` until a real event is added.
To add one:

1. Find a documented flood event for your target ward (e.g. a municipal
   disaster-management report, a news report with a specific date/location,
   or IMD rainfall records) with a source URL.
2. Add a `HistoricalEvent` to `backend/app/ml/backtest.py`'s `EVENTS` dict
   with real `date`, `location_note`, `rainfall_timeseries_mm_hr`,
   `reported_flooded_streets`, and `source_url` values.
3. `run_backtest()` is already fully wired to run it through the same
   prediction pipeline used for live nowcasting.

## Adding a real pilot ward

1. Pick a bounding box (`min_lon,min_lat,max_lon,max_lat`) for your target
   ward (~2km x 2km recommended, matching the synthetic ward's scale).
2. Set `PILOT_MODE=real` and `REAL_WARD_BBOX=<your bbox>` in `.env`.
3. Restart the backend. On first boot, `RealWardProvider` automatically:
   - fetches real elevation data for the bbox from AWS's public terrarium
     tiles (`app/gis/terrain_tiles.py`) and caches it to `backend/data/`,
   - pulls the real road network, waterways, and critical infrastructure
     from OpenStreetMap via `osmnx`,
   - trains a GNN checkpoint against that real topology (a few seconds;
     cached at `app/ml/checkpoints/real/` for subsequent boots).
   No manual downloads or accounts are required. This needs network
   access; if you have a higher-resolution SRTM tile from USGS
   EarthExplorer instead (`scripts/download_srtm.py` documents that path),
   drop it at `backend/data/real_ward_dem.tif` before first boot and it
   will be used instead of the automated fetch.
4. If your bbox has few or no OSM-tagged hospitals/fire
   stations/shelters, `/api/critical-infrastructure` will honestly return
   fewer than 3 (or zero) -- nothing is invented to fill the gap.
